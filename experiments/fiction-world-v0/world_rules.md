# World Rules

Thirty load-bearing rules of the Lantern-Keepers' world, each stated explicitly and then expanded with two to four example or paraphrase sentences. These are the rules whose causal structure forms the cycles that ACS detection should surface.

## R1 — Lighting a lantern produces mist-light

When a lantern is lit with mire-essence, it produces mist-light. A freshly trimmed lantern wick soaked in mire-essence will burn with a pale silver flame the Keepers call mist-light. Mist-light is the visible product of mire-essence combustion, and no other fuel produces it. Without mire-essence, a lantern can burn ordinary oil, but it gives only a yellow flame and no mist-light at all.

## R2 — Mist-light attracts nightmoths

Mist-light visible at night attracts nightmoths from the cliff air and the Hollows. Nightmoths cannot see ordinary firelight, but they orient toward mist-light from a great distance. On a clear autumn night, a single lit lantern at the Cliff Path will draw between ten and forty nightmoths over the course of a single watch. Nightmoths fly toward mist-light even in cold winds, though their numbers fall in true cold.

## R3 — Nightmoths landing on lanterns leave silken threads

When nightmoths land on a lantern's rim, some of them leave fine silken threads stuck to the warm stone. The threads come off the moths' undersides when they rest. A lantern will accumulate threads over the course of a night so long as moths are landing. By dawn, a well-attended lantern with a strong moth-draw will hold a small grey nest of these threads on its lip.

## R4 — Silken threads boiled in sea-water yield mire-essence

When silken threads are collected and boiled in sea-water in a copper cauldron at Cauldron Hall, mire-essence forms in the cauldron. The threads dissolve into a thin slick that settles at the bottom of the cauldron after several hours of slow boil. The slick is then skimmed into clay vessels and aged. The sea-water must come from the Salt-bloom Tide-pools or from the harbor's deep ebb to work. Fresh water will not produce mire-essence, no matter how long the threads are boiled.

## R5 — Distilled mire-essence is stored and feeds future lighting

When mire-essence forms in the cauldron, it is harvested and stored in clay vessels in the cellars of Cauldron Hall. The stored mire-essence is what the Keepers draw from when they tend lanterns. Mire-essence in clay vessels remains good for at least three years. Each lantern uses roughly a thumb's depth of mire-essence per night of burning. (This rule closes Cycle A: lantern → mist-light → moths → threads → mire-essence → lantern.)

## R6 — Above twenty moths per lantern, sky-cats descend

When the nightmoth population on a section of lantern-row exceeds twenty per night, sky-cats descend from the cliff-spires to hunt them. Sky-cats are silent fliers and the Keepers usually do not see them arrive. The threshold of twenty is a rough rule of thumb used by Keepers when they keep moth-counts. Below twenty moths, sky-cats remain on the spires and do not hunt that night.

## R7 — Sky-cats hunting reduces nightmoth populations

When sky-cats descend, they hunt nightmoths and reduce the nightmoth population on that section of lantern-row. A single sky-cat will take between fifteen and thirty moths in a night of hunting. After a sky-cat hunt, the silken-thread harvest from those lanterns drops sharply for the next two or three nights.

## R8 — Sky-cat presence makes nightmoths flee to dimmer lights

When sky-cats are present at a lantern-row, surviving nightmoths flee toward dimmer lights nearby. The fleeing moths often relocate to lanterns in the inland Hollows, which are less brightly burned and offer less mist-light. As a consequence, sky-cat presence on the Cliff Path can briefly enrich the Hollows lanterns with extra threads.

## R9 — Sky-cats roost on the cliff-spires by day

Sky-cats roost on the cliff-spires above Aelmere during daylight hours. They do not enter the village or its buildings. The spires are a row of weathered stone columns above the eastern cliff, where the wind blows steadily. The Keepers consider sky-cats wild but tolerated, and have never tried to drive them off.

## R10 — Sky-cats molt feathers to the cliff base

When sky-cats molt, their feathers drift down to the cliff base below the spires. The feather-fall is heaviest in late summer and early autumn after a busy hunting season. Feathers are collected by appointed apprentices each morning during molting weeks. The accumulated feathers are stored in a sealed bin at the Watch.

## R11 — Un-tended lanterns die before dawn

When a lantern is left un-tended overnight, its flame dies before dawn. A Keeper is expected to refresh the mire-essence and trim the wick twice between dusk and dawn. A lantern with a fresh trimming and full essence might last most of one watch, but no lantern burns through a full night without intervention. An un-tended lantern is one of the most serious failures a Keeper can be charged with.

## R12 — Un-tended lanterns at the Sunken Cove raise wraiths

When a lantern at the Sunken Cove dies before dawn, a wraith emerges from the cove waters by the next dark moon. This rule applies only to lanterns at the Sunken Cove, and not to lanterns elsewhere on the Cliff Path. The Sunken Cove is the village's old graveyard, drowned in a storm-surge six generations ago, and the Keepers believe the wraiths are the unsettled dead from that disaster. Wraith emergence is the worst event the village fears.

## R13 — Wraiths drain mire-essence from lit lanterns

When a wraith approaches a lit lantern, it drains the mire-essence from the lantern's reservoir, dimming the flame. The drain is not visible to the eye, but a Keeper finds the lantern's essence-vessel half-empty after a wraith passes. A drained lantern continues to burn for a time on what remains, but at greatly reduced mist-light.

## R14 — Drained lanterns burn weakly and attract fewer moths

When a lantern has been drained by a wraith, its flame is dim and its mist-light is weak. Weak mist-light draws far fewer nightmoths — perhaps a quarter of the usual number. A run of wraith-drained lanterns on the Cliff Path can therefore reduce the night's silken-thread harvest substantially. (This is Cycle D's closure: un-tended → wraith → drained → less mist-light → less production.)

## R15 — Ground sky-cat feathers ward lanterns from wraiths

When ground sky-cat feathers are scattered around the base of a lit lantern, wraiths cannot approach that lantern. The feathers must be freshly ground; older preparations lose their power within a few weeks. One handful of ground feathers protects a single lantern for about a month before requiring replenishment. (This is Cycle C's hinge: sky-cat byproduct enables safe lantern-tending in the wraith-prone zone.)

## R16 — Cold winds extinguish unprotected lanterns

When the sea-breeze drops below the cold-threshold at night, lanterns extinguish unless they have been prepared for cold. The cold-threshold is what the Keepers call the wind that comes off the Cold Sea on clear winter nights, sharp enough to chill standing water in minutes. Lanterns on the exposed Cliff Path are most at risk. Lanterns in cliff-shielded coves are largely safe.

## R17 — Salt-bloom in winter slows the burn

When salt-bloom is added to mire-essence in winter weeks, the mire-essence burns more slowly and a lantern can survive cold winds. The Keepers add a pinch of salt-bloom to each lantern's essence-vessel from the first hard frost through the last spring storm. A lantern with salt-bloom in its essence burns at perhaps three-quarters the rate of a lantern without, and resists the cold-threshold wind for the length of a watch.

## R18 — Salt-bloom is harvested at new moon

When salt-bloom is harvested from the Salt-bloom Tide-pools at the new moon, it retains its potency. Salt-bloom harvested at other phases turns to chalky residue within a week. The new-moon harvest is one of the village's important monthly observances, and is led by the Salt-bloom Warden (a rotating duty among Keepers).

## R19 — Salt-bloom blooms in tide-pools at spring tides

When the tide-pools fill during a spring tide, salt-bloom blooms within them. Salt-bloom is a pale crystalline encrustation that grows on the underside of submerged tide-pool stones. The bloom is harvestable for only a few days after the tide-pool fills, before the bloom dissolves back into the seawater.

## R20 — Spring tides occur near new moon and full moon

Spring tides — the largest tides of the lunar cycle — occur near the new moon and the full moon. The salt-bloom new-moon harvest is timed to a spring tide. The full-moon spring tide is not used for salt-bloom (because salt-bloom harvested then is unstable) but is observed by the Council as a separate calendar marker.

## R21 — Wintergloss herb repels nightmoths

When wintergloss herb is burned alongside mire-essence in a lantern, nightmoths are repelled from that lantern. Wintergloss is rarely burned, because the village depends on attracting nightmoths. The exception is at the Stilllight Lantern at the Sunken Cove, where during certain weeks the Council orders wintergloss burned to keep moth-counts below the threshold that would otherwise draw sky-cats into the cove (where the wraiths sometimes harm them).

## R22 — Cliff-spires shield the Northcove from cold winds

When cliff-spires shield a cove from the north winds, lanterns in that cove burn stable through winter. The Northcove is the only such cove in Aelmere's geography. Lanterns in the Northcove never need salt-bloom and are never lost to cold winds. The Northcove is therefore the village's most productive lantern-row and its most reliable source of silken threads during difficult winters.

## R23 — Keepers inherit lantern-stations

When a Keeper inherits a lantern-station, they take responsibility for that station's tending. A station is typically inherited from a parent or aunt at the senior Keeper's retirement or death. The inherited Keeper may not transfer the station outside the family without Council approval. Most stations have been in the same family for at least three generations.

## R24 — Newcomers apprentice for one full year

When a newcomer wishes to become a Keeper, they must apprentice for one full year under a senior Keeper. The apprentice does not tend any lantern alone for the first six months. The Council decides whether an apprentice is permitted to begin solo tending after the half-year mark.

## R25 — Thirty consecutive solo nights complete apprenticeship

When an apprentice has lit and tended their assigned lantern alone for thirty consecutive nights without failure, they complete apprenticeship and may be invested as a Keeper. A single dim-flame event during these thirty nights resets the count. A failed lantern (one that dies before dawn) also resets the count.

## R26 — The Council convenes at full moon

When the moon is full, the Council of Keepers convenes at the Watch. The Council meets at sundown and may sit through the night if business requires. The Council does not meet outside full moons except in declared emergency.

## R27 — The Council redistributes mire-essence at each meeting

When the Council convenes, mire-essence reserves are reviewed and redistributed across lantern-stations. Stations with high production (such as the Northcove) contribute to a central pool. Stations with low production or special burdens (such as the Sunken Cove) draw from it. The redistribution is recorded in the Watch's ledgers.

## R28 — Heavy sky-cat hunting in summer yields heavy feather harvest in autumn

When sky-cats hunt frequently through summer, more feathers accumulate at the cliff base by autumn. The connection is mechanical: more hunting means more molting, and molting feathers fall during the hunting weeks. A summer of strong moth-draw and frequent sky-cat descents is therefore directly correlated with the village's autumn feather store.

## R29 — Large feather stores enable safe Sunken-Cove tending

When the feather store at the Watch is large, the Keepers can safely tend lanterns at the Sunken Cove during winter, when wraith risk is highest. The Council issues feather rations from the store to the Sunken-Cove station each winter month. A small feather store means rationing, missed wardings, and increased wraith risk.

## R30 — High wraith activity prompts feather-ration requests

When wraith activity at the Sunken Cove increases, neighboring lantern-stations request additional feather rations from the Council. The Council weighs requests against the central feather store at each meeting. Stations that report wraith sightings or drained-lantern events are prioritized for rations.
