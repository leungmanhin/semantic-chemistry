# Event Narratives

Specific event narratives demonstrating the world rules in action. These are intended to give pattern-mining and PLN inference multiple concrete instances of each rule, and to provide the event-log substrate from which ACSs can be detected. Time markers are relative to the autumn equinox of Year 1.

---

## Part I. The Equinox Week

### Two days before the equinox

Coraline Ash arrived in Aelmere on a third-day market boat from down the coast. She was twenty-one, with sun-bleached hair and the calloused hands of a farmgirl. Old Vesh met her at the harbor and walked her up to the Watch.

Coraline's audience with the Council lasted most of the afternoon. The Council asked her about her family, her health, her reasons for coming, and her willingness to commit to a full year of apprenticeship with no guarantee of investment. She answered carefully. The Council voted to accept her after Old Vesh observed that the village had no Keeper-family apprentice candidates ready for this year's intake.

Coraline was assigned to Pell as her senior. Pell was nineteen and only just out of her own apprenticeship. The pairing was unusual but the Council judged that Pell's experience was fresh enough to be useful and that the age difference would make the apprenticeship less formal than usual.

Coraline did not light a lantern on her first night. She walked the Cliff Path with Pell from sundown to dawn, carrying Pell's leather case and watching Pell trim, fill, and light. She asked few questions. Pell explained the order of operations as she worked: clean glass, trim wick, fill essence-vessel, strike. Coraline copied each step in her notebook.

### One day before the equinox

The salt-bloom store at Cauldron Hall held enough for two and a half months of regular winter use. Hesper noted in his weekly report that the previous new-moon harvest had been thin. The Council would discuss salt-bloom planning at the next full moon.

Meren Tallowhand walked her Cliff Path station at sundown. Joren Salt walked her up the path before turning to his own evening's fishing duties. Meren's lantern lit cleanly. Moth-count by the third watch was seventeen — below the sky-cat threshold of twenty.

Bevin Coombe walked the Cove Stair at sundown. He touched the Stilllight's iron bands and lit the lantern at the head of the Stair. The Stilllight burned through the night without incident. Bevin walked the Stair again at midnight, as he always did, and again at the third watch.

The autumn equinox is the formal start of moth-season. Marra Tallowhand's logs noted moths beginning to appear in significant numbers from this date.

### The equinox night

The autumn equinox night was clear and mild. Moth-counts across the Cliff Path averaged twenty-five per lantern. Three sky-cats were observed descending to hunt on the eastern stretch of the path. Their hunt reduced moth-counts on that stretch to seven by the third watch.

Coraline carried Pell's case again. They watched the sky-cat hunt from a distance. Pell explained how the moth-count threshold drove sky-cat descent. Coraline asked: "Where do the moths go when they flee from sky-cats?" Pell answered: "Mostly to the Hollows lanterns, which are dimmer." Coraline wrote this down.

The next morning Tessa Brae and her two assistants walked the cliff base and collected forty-one molted feathers. This was a strong haul, consistent with active hunting the previous night. The feathers were weighed, recorded, and added to the Watch bin.

Cauldron Hall began a distillation cycle that morning. Hesper filled all three cauldrons with sea-water from the Salt-bloom Tide-pools store, added the previous night's threads (collected from the lanterns by Pell and other senior Keepers on dawn-out), and brought the cauldrons to a slow boil. By late afternoon the slick was visible at the bottom of each cauldron. The skim produced enough mire-essence to fill three clay vessels.

## Part II. The First Cold Snap

### Sixteen days after the equinox

The first cold snap of the year came earlier than usual. The sea-breeze dropped to cold-threshold for the first time in autumn, well before the typical first hard frost. Eleven lanterns on the exposed mid-Cliff-Path stretch died before dawn. The Keepers had not yet begun adding salt-bloom to the mire-essence, because the season had not formally started.

Five of those lanterns were on stations tended by senior Keepers who had not anticipated the cold. The other six were on stations tended by less senior Keepers. None of them were at the Sunken Cove. None of them were in the Northcove (which was as always sheltered).

The Council called an emergency meeting the next afternoon. The decision was to begin salt-bloom additions immediately, drawing from the existing store. Hesper distributed salt-bloom pouches to all Cliff Path Keepers. Northcove Keepers were instructed they did not need to take salt-bloom yet. The Council recorded the emergency cold snap in the ledger.

Salt-bloom additions began that same night. No lanterns died on the second night of the cold snap. The cold snap broke after three nights, but the Council kept salt-bloom in distribution for the rest of the autumn.

The thin lanterns of the previous night had also drawn fewer moths. Marra's logs the next morning showed an average moth-count of twelve on the mid-Cliff-Path stretch, down from twenty-five on the equinox night. This was the first observable instance in the year of Cycle D — the wraith-counter-cycle pattern in which dim lanterns produce less mist-light and therefore fewer moths and therefore fewer threads — though no wraith was responsible for the dimness here, only the cold.

The thin moth-count translated to a thin thread harvest. The next morning Pell and Coraline collected silken threads from the mid-Cliff-Path lanterns; the haul was perhaps a third of what they had been collecting on equinox nights. Cauldron Hall's distillation that day produced two vessels rather than three.

Coraline asked Pell at midnight: "If the lanterns burn weaker, do we lose moths, and if we lose moths do we lose threads, and if we lose threads do we lose essence?" Pell answered yes to all three. Coraline noted in her notebook: "lantern → moths → threads → essence. Closed loop."

### Twenty-one days after the equinox

The first full moon since Coraline's arrival. Council met. The redistribution this month favored the Cliff Path stretch that had lost lanterns: extra essence was issued to those stations for the coming month, drawn from the central pool that the Northcove had contributed to.

Hesper reported that the cold snap had cost approximately one and a half vessels of production. This was within recoverable range but the year was beginning short. The salt-bloom store was now eight weeks of supply at the increased winter rate.

Brindle Coombe reported the Northcove production was as expected. The Northcove had not been affected by the cold snap at all, because the cliff-spires shielded the cove from the north wind.

Sailsworn reported on the upcoming new-moon harvest. The next new moon was twelve days away. The harvest crew would be twelve people, the standard size.

Old Vesh suggested that Coraline accompany the salt-bloom harvest expedition. The Council agreed.

## Part III. The First Salt-bloom Harvest

### Thirty-three days after the equinox: new-moon night

Coraline walked with the salt-bloom harvest expedition to the Salt-bloom Tide-pools. The party left at sundown, in waxed coats, carrying copper buckets and wooden scrapers. Sailsworn led the party along the cliff-top goat-path.

The new moon was a thin sliver, almost invisible. The tide was high — a spring tide, the highest of the cycle. The tide-pools were full and overflowing as the party arrived.

Sailsworn assigned each member of the party to one of the five blooming pools. Coraline was assigned to the second pool from the north. She knelt at the pool's edge, reached carefully under the cold water, and ran the wooden scraper along the submerged stones. The salt-bloom came off in pale flakes that floated to the surface, where she gathered them with a fine net.

The harvest took the full night. The party rotated pools at midnight and again at the third watch. The southernmost two pools were inspected as a matter of course and confirmed not blooming, as expected.

By dawn the party returned to Cauldron Hall with twelve full buckets of salt-bloom — a strong harvest, much heavier than the previous new-moon harvest had been. Hesper poured the buckets directly into the salt-bloom store cabinet and labeled each compartment with the date.

The Council took the harvest report at the next full moon and noted that the salt-bloom store was now fourteen weeks of winter supply. The earlier shortfall had been recovered.

### Forty-seven days after the equinox: the Stilllight ration

The next full-moon Council meeting issued the standard winter feather ration to Bevin Coombe at the Stilllight. Bevin reported no wraith activity. The feather store was at eight weeks of supply, which Tessa noted was on the low side for this time of year.

The Council adjusted ration policy slightly: extra feathers would be issued to the Stilllight and the two cliff-path stations nearest the Sunken Cove for the coming month, to ensure warding through the early winter.

## Part IV. The Sky-cat Drought

### Sixty days after the equinox

The Keepers noticed that sky-cat numbers on the spires had been low for two weeks. Tessa's feather collection had been thin: twelve feathers per morning, instead of the usual thirty to forty for this time of year. Marra and several other Keepers had observed only three or four sky-cats visible on the spires by morning, rather than the usual eight to twelve.

This was discussed at the next full-moon Council. The conclusion was that the sky-cat population had reduced — possibly because of a thin moth-year overall, possibly because some sky-cats had moved up the coast in search of better hunting. The Council had no way to verify.

The thin feather harvest meant the autumn feather store would be smaller than usual. Tessa projected the autumn total at less than three-quarters of the usual amount. The Council discussed whether to ration feathers more strictly in the coming winter.

Old Vesh observed that during the last bad winter (twenty-six years ago), the autumn before had also had a thin feather harvest. The Council noted this in the ledger.

Coraline asked Pell: "Why does fewer sky-cats mean fewer feathers?" Pell explained that sky-cats molt while hunting, so fewer sky-cats hunting meant fewer feathers falling. Coraline noted: "sky-cats → hunting → feather drop. If we lose sky-cats we lose feathers, and feathers ward wraiths."

The next morning Pell asked Old Vesh: "If feather supply drops and the Sunken Cove can't be well warded, what should we do about the Stilllight?" Old Vesh answered: "We tend it more carefully. We don't let it dim, ever."

### Seventy-five days after the equinox

A small follow-up: a single sky-cat was observed hunting at the Sunken Cove on a clear night. This was unusual; sky-cats normally hunted Cliff Path stretches and the Hollows. The Council considered ordering wintergloss burned at the Stilllight to keep moth-counts below the sky-cat threshold, but decided against it for now, as overall moth-counts in the cove were already low (because the Stilllight was the only lantern in the cove and didn't draw moths to threshold-crossing density on its own).

### Eighty days after the equinox

Coraline had been carrying Pell's case for almost three months. The Council approved her transition to half-shifts at her own station: a Cliff Path station near the harbor mouth that had been temporarily unstaffed since a senior Keeper's retirement. Coraline would tend the station with Pell joining at midnight and the third watch.

Coraline lit the lantern for the first time on the eightieth night after the equinox. Pell stood at her shoulder. The wick caught cleanly. Mist-light spread from the dome. Coraline noted the first moth landing at thirteen minutes past sundown.

### Eighty-five days after the equinox

Pell observed that the Hollows lanterns had been receiving an unusual boost in moths. The cause was probably the thinness of sky-cat hunting on the Cliff Path: with fewer sky-cats descending, fewer moths were being displaced to the Hollows, so the Hollows boost should have been smaller — but the Cliff Path lanterns were drawing fewer moths overall in this thin year, so the Hollows moths were finding richer pickings on the Cliff Path itself and many were actually moving outward, not inward. Pell wrote a note about this for the Council's next consideration.

The thread harvest from Hollows lanterns increased by perhaps fifteen percent in the eighty-fifth-day week. The thread harvest from the Cliff Path remained thin.

## Part V. First Frost and Mid-Winter

### Ninety-two days after the equinox: first hard frost

The first hard frost arrived. By morning the harbor was rimed white. The cold-threshold wind had blown all night. All Cliff Path lanterns had salt-bloom in their essence and burned through the night without loss.

The Council formally opened the salt-bloom season at the next full-moon meeting. Salt-bloom rations were standardized for the winter. The Northcove was exempt as always.

The Cauldron Hall distillation continued through frost weeks but at reduced volume, because the thread harvest was thin. Hesper expected approximately three months of essence production at current rates would yield perhaps eight to nine vessels — less than the usual fifteen to seventeen for the cold months.

Brindle Coombe noted at Council: "We are behind on essence and behind on feathers. If we have a hard winter we will be making decisions about which lanterns to dim."

Old Vesh responded: "We do not dim the Stilllight."

### One hundred and eleven days after the equinox: Lantern-night

The longest night of the year, Lantern-night, was clear and very cold. Every lantern in the village was lit. The Stilllight was lit by Meren Tallowhand at sundown — she did the honors that year as the Council speaker.

The whole village walked the Cliff Path at midnight from the harbor wall to the cliff-spires. The cold-threshold wind was strong but every lantern held. Coraline walked at Pell's shoulder. Joren Salt walked with Meren.

Stories were told at the harbor fires until dawn. The most-repeated story was again of the worst winter sixty-three years ago. Old Vesh told a shorter version this year, with the detail of his father walking the Cove Stair.

Three sky-cats were visible on the spires by morning. Tessa noted in her log: "fewer than usual for Lantern-night."

### One hundred and twenty days after the equinox

A wraith emerged from the Sunken Cove.

The trigger was a dim-flame event at the Stilllight nine days before — Bevin Coombe had been ill that night and a substitute Keeper had refilled the essence at midnight but had not noticed that the wick was carbonized. The lantern had burned dim for three hours, neither extinguished nor at full mist-light. Council later judged this as equivalent to an un-tended event.

The wraith was first seen by Marra Tallowhand on her station near the Cove path. She walked to the Stilllight to find Bevin already there, scattering feathers around the base. The wraith approached, paused, and turned away. It did not drain the Stilllight.

The wraith persisted for eleven days. During its persistence, six Cliff Path lanterns near the cove were drained on various nights. The drained lanterns burned weakly the rest of those nights, with mist-light at perhaps a quarter of normal strength. Moth-counts at those lanterns fell from twenty-three on average to six on average. The week's thread harvest from those stations was approximately one-sixth of the previous week's.

The Council met in emergency session twice during the wraith's persistence. Feather rations were doubled at the affected stations. The substitute Keeper who had failed at the Stilllight was brought before Council and reprimanded; Bevin defended him on grounds of inexperience and Council issued a written warning rather than a station-bar.

The wraith faded back into the cove on the morning of the eleventh day. The drained lanterns gradually returned to normal mist-light over the following week as their essence reserves were refilled by Hesper.

### One hundred and thirty-one days after the equinox

A second cold snap. Three Cliff Path lanterns died despite salt-bloom. The cold-threshold this time was unusually sharp — the sea-breeze was estimated by Old Vesh to be below cold-threshold by a significant margin, beyond what salt-bloom could compensate for. The Council formally noted this as a worse-than-cold-threshold event.

Tessa's feather store was now at four weeks of supply. Half the autumn store had been used in the wraith response.

## Part VI. The Second New-moon Harvest

### One hundred and forty-five days after the equinox

The next salt-bloom new-moon harvest. The party went as scheduled. The tide-pools were full. The harvest was again strong — eleven buckets, slightly less than the equinox-week harvest.

Coraline went again, this time also carrying her own bucket and scraper. She harvested from the third pool from the north. She filled her bucket by the third watch.

The harvest brought salt-bloom store back to ten weeks of winter supply. The Council noted that with stricter rationing the supply might stretch through the cold season.

### One hundred and fifty-five days after the equinox

Tessa Brae reported that sky-cat sightings had begun to recover slightly. Six sky-cats were now visible on the spires by morning. Feather harvest had risen to twenty per morning.

The Council expressed cautious optimism. The wraith had been warded successfully, salt-bloom supplies were adequate, and sky-cats appeared to be returning.

Bevin Coombe asked at Council whether the substitute Keeper from the wraith event should be considered for permanent assignment to a station; he had performed well as the Stilllight substitute since then. Council approved an apprenticeship-completion track for him.

---

## Part VII. Mid-Spring Recovery

### Two hundred and ten days after the equinox: spring equinox

The spring equinox. The salt-bloom season formally closed at the next full-moon Council meeting. The Northcove resumed its usual production. Cliff Path stations stepped down salt-bloom additions over the following two weeks.

Cauldron Hall began a heavier distillation cycle as thread harvests returned to normal-spring levels. By late spring the essence store had been partly replenished.

Tessa's feather store ended the winter at three weeks of supply — perilously low, but not exhausted. Sky-cat sightings had returned to near-normal levels by spring equinox.

### Two hundred and thirty days after the equinox

Coraline began her thirty-night solo stretch — the final requirement for apprenticeship completion. Her station was the Cliff Path station near the harbor mouth that she had been half-tending for several months. Pell did not accompany her, but visited on her own off-nights.

Coraline lit her lantern each evening at sundown, refreshed essence at midnight, trimmed at the third watch, and extinguished at dawn. She kept her notebook carefully.

On the seventeenth night of her solo stretch, a thin fog rolled in off the sea and reduced visibility on the Cliff Path. Coraline kept her lantern burning steadily. The thread harvest the next morning was good — perhaps thirty-five threads from her lantern alone.

On the twenty-second night, the cold-threshold wind returned briefly. Coraline had salt-bloom in her essence (the Council had not yet stepped down all stations) and the lantern held.

On the twenty-ninth night, three sky-cats descended on the Cliff Path near her station. Coraline observed the hunt and made notes. The moth-count at her lantern dropped from twenty-four to nine over the course of the hunt.

On the thirtieth night, Coraline lit her lantern at sundown for the thirtieth consecutive night without failure. Pell met her at dawn. They walked back to the village together. The Council met later that day in informal session — not waiting for the full moon — to formally invest Coraline as a Keeper.

The investment took place at sundown. Coraline lit her first lantern as a full Keeper. Meren placed a hand on her shoulder and said: "The lanterns must burn." The phrase was repeated by everyone present.

### Two hundred and seventy days after the equinox

The year's first summer feast was held at midsummer. The Keepers ate together at the village square at midday. The Council toasted Coraline's investment along with Mira's, who had completed her own apprenticeship at the solstice.

Meren noted privately to Old Vesh that the year had been a difficult one but not a bad-winter. They had lost no lanterns to wraith activity that the wraith itself had not drained (and the drained lanterns had recovered). They had lost three lanterns total to cold snaps. Their feather store had nearly run out but had held.

Old Vesh agreed but observed that the village had been close to a bad winter, and that the sky-cat drought of the autumn had been the precipitating cause. He recommended that the Council propose to the village a small additional measure: a few specific Cliff Path stations should burn slightly brighter (with extra mire-essence ration) during early winter to draw more moths and therefore retain more sky-cats. Council took this under consideration for the next full moon.

## Part VIII. The Following Autumn

### Three hundred and sixty-five days after the original equinox: second autumn equinox

The second autumn equinox, one full year on. The feather store was at eight weeks of supply, having recovered through the busy summer. Sky-cat numbers were back to ten visible on the spires by morning. The salt-bloom store was at eleven weeks for the upcoming winter, which was a normal supply. The mire-essence store was at twelve weeks below the usual reserve level, still recovering from the previous year's losses.

Coraline was now in her second year as a Keeper. She had inherited the station she had completed her solo stretch on. She wrote her own log nightly now.

Pell had taken on a new apprentice — a young man from a Keeper family named Roben. Pell carried the role with practiced ease.

The Council at the autumn-equinox full-moon meeting accepted Old Vesh's brighter-station proposal. Three Cliff Path stations were assigned extra essence rations for early winter, with the explicit goal of drawing higher moth counts and retaining sky-cats.

### Three hundred and ninety-five days after the original equinox

The first cold snap of the second year arrived right on time. Salt-bloom additions were already in place across the Cliff Path. No lanterns were lost. Moth-counts on the brighter stations averaged thirty per night, above the sky-cat threshold. Sky-cat hunts were observed on six of the next ten nights. Feather harvest the following month was strong.

Coraline noted in her log: "lantern brighter → more moths → more sky-cats → more feathers → safer Sunken Cove."

She read the note to Pell over breakfast. Pell laughed and said: "You've worked out the village in your notebook."

## Part IX. Smaller Incidents Through Year 2

### Day 410 of the corpus

A single lantern at the mid-Cliff-Path was extinguished by a sudden gust during a calm evening. The Keeper relit it within the hour, before any wraith-relevant interval had passed. The lantern was not at the Sunken Cove, so no wraith risk arose. The Council was notified at the next morning's informal meeting and the incident was added to the ledger as a minor weather event.

The Keeper who relit the lantern was Marra Tallowhand. She had observed the gust from her own station nearby and walked over to assist. Marra noted in her log that the Cliff Path had several stretches where wind funneling could cause similar incidents, and recommended that the Council issue extra essence ration to those specific lanterns. The Council took the recommendation under advisement.

### Day 425 of the corpus: a thread harvest experiment

Hesper proposed a small experiment at Cauldron Hall: distill silken threads in three different water sources — Salt-bloom Tide-pools water, deep-ebb water, and ordinary harbor water from mid-pier. The proposal was approved by the Council. Three cauldrons were filled, one each.

After a full day's slow boil and skim, the Salt-bloom Tide-pools cauldron produced one full vessel of mire-essence. The deep-ebb cauldron produced one full vessel, perhaps slightly less. The harbor-mid-pier cauldron produced no mire-essence at all — only a chalky residue at the bottom.

Hesper reported the results to Council. The deep-ebb and tide-pools sources were confirmed effective. Mid-pier harbor water was confirmed ineffective. This was already the working understanding of the Cauldron Hall Wardens, but Hesper's experiment provided documented evidence. The result was added to the ledger.

### Day 440 of the corpus: a feather-collection anomaly

Tessa Brae reported an unusual feather find at the cliff base: a large sky-cat feather (longer than typical) was found near spire-three. The feather was added to the store but Tessa noted that it might indicate an unusually large sky-cat, possibly a new arrival from elsewhere on the coast.

The Council noted the observation but took no action. Sky-cat individuals are not tracked by name, and feather-by-feather identification is not part of normal village practice.

### Day 455 of the corpus: an apprentice begins

A second new apprentice arrived in Year 2, a girl from a Keeper family named Eli. Eli was assigned to a senior Keeper named Norren, who is a Council member but had not previously taken an apprentice. The pairing was straightforward and the Council recorded it without dissent.

Eli began carrying Norren's case on her second night. Her first six months would be conventional — no solo tending, full senior supervision.

### Day 470 of the corpus: a snowfall

The first snowfall of Year 2 arrived on day 470. It was a light snow that fell through the early evening and was gone by dawn. Lanterns burned normally throughout. The cold-threshold wind did not accompany the snow.

Coraline noted in her log that the snow made the mist-light visible at longer distances than usual, because of reflection off the white-coated cliffs. Moth-counts at her station were higher than typical for that week — perhaps a snow-mist-light visibility effect, though she could not be sure.

### Day 485 of the corpus: a missed midnight check

A junior Keeper named Wynn missed his midnight check at his Cliff Path station due to a household emergency (a sick child). He returned to his station at the third watch and found the lantern burning low. He refilled the essence and trimmed the wick, and the lantern recovered. The lantern was not at the Sunken Cove.

Wynn reported the incident to Council the next morning. The Council issued no penalty given the legitimate emergency and Wynn's prompt recovery action. The incident was logged.

### Day 495 of the corpus: a wraith near-miss

A possible wraith sighting was reported by Bevin Coombe at the Stilllight, on a night when the lantern had been burning steadily. Bevin reported a brief glimpse of a pale shape near the cove waters that turned away before approaching the Stilllight. Bevin had freshly applied ground feathers around the Stilllight base earlier that evening.

The Council convened an emergency session the next afternoon. Bevin's report was logged as a "possible wraith near-miss." The Council's decision was to maintain the doubled feather-ration at the Stilllight through the next two months and to instruct nearby Cliff Path Keepers to be alert.

No further sightings followed. The incident did not develop into a full wraith persistence event. The Council later judged that the feather warding had functioned as designed and prevented the wraith from emerging fully.

### Day 510 of the corpus: a longer cold-threshold

A two-week stretch of cold-threshold winds arrived in mid-winter Year 2. All Cliff Path lanterns held throughout, thanks to salt-bloom additions. The Northcove was unaffected as usual.

Mire-essence usage was higher than average during the stretch because the salt-bloom-slowed lanterns still required refilling at midnight and the third watch, and the Keepers used slightly more salt-bloom per lantern than the standard ration to compensate for the unusually sharp wind. The Council adjusted the salt-bloom ration upward by ten percent for the duration.

Thread harvest from the Cliff Path was steady through the stretch, because moths continued to fly in the cold-threshold wind (though in smaller numbers — perhaps half of the usual count). Cauldron Hall continued to distill at normal cadence.

### Day 525 of the corpus: a half-investiture

The third-year apprentice from Year 1, Mira, who had been invested earlier, took her first solo new-moon harvest shift as a Keeper. She led a small party of two assistants to the Salt-bloom Tide-pools. The harvest went smoothly. The bucket count was nine, somewhat under the typical eleven, but the season's salt-bloom store was already well stocked from earlier harvests.

Mira reported to Council the next morning. The Council congratulated her on her first lead. The harvest was added to the store and the date recorded.

## Part X. Spring of Year 2

### Day 540 of the corpus: the spring equinox approaches

The Council met at the full moon preceding spring equinox. The salt-bloom season was nearing its formal close. The store remained at twelve weeks of supply — comfortable, because the cold-threshold stretches had been weathered without breakage of the supply chain.

Tessa reported the feather store at five weeks of supply. The figure was lower than ideal but reflected the heavy use through Year 2's winter near-miss and the doubled Stilllight ration. The Council resolved to continue the doubled Stilllight ration through the spring and to issue a clear request to apprentices to attend the morning feather-collection more thoroughly through molting weeks of the coming summer.

Mire-essence reserves were at eight months of supply — recovered substantially from the lows of Year 1, though still below the historical mark of twelve months. Hesper noted that with a strong summer thread harvest, the reserve could return to twelve months by the second winter.

### Day 555 of the corpus: spring equinox

The spring equinox passed clear and warm. The salt-bloom season formally closed at the next full-moon meeting. All Cliff Path stations stepped down salt-bloom additions over the following two weeks. The Northcove returned to its summer cadence.

Cauldron Hall ramped up distillation in expectation of stronger thread harvests. Hesper assigned an extra apprentice to morning thread-collection so that more lanterns' threads could be brought in faster.

### Day 575 of the corpus: a sky-cat census

The Council attempted an informal sky-cat census on a morning when the spires were unusually clearly visible from the village. Several Keepers walked to the cliff base and counted sky-cats visible on each spire. The total count was fourteen, which was slightly higher than the previous year's estimate of twelve.

The Council interpreted this as a small recovery of the sky-cat population, consistent with the strong feather harvests observed through Year 2. The census was logged as an estimate, not a precise count.

### Day 590 of the corpus: an apprentice question

Coraline, by now in her second year as a Keeper and beginning to take occasional Council questions herself, was asked by the new apprentice Eli: "Why do we add salt-bloom only at the Cliff Path and not at the Northcove?"

Coraline answered: "Because salt-bloom slows the burn rate, which helps the lantern survive cold-threshold winds. The Northcove is shielded from those winds by the cliff-spires, so its lanterns don't need the slow-burn protection. Salt-bloom is a scarce resource — we use it only where needed."

Eli wrote the answer in her own notebook. Coraline added in passing: "Also, lanterns burning at full rate produce more mist-light, and more mist-light means more moths and more threads. The Northcove is therefore our most productive row — we don't want to slow it down."

### Day 605 of the corpus: a fishing storm

A spring storm hit the harbor in early evening. Three fishing boats were caught at sea and made for the harbor at full speed. They followed the harbor wall lanterns, which burned through the storm because the Keepers on duty refilled essence early and adjusted the lantern shielding against the wind.

All three boats reached harbor safely. Joren Salt was on one of them and arrived at Meren's station just before dawn, soaked through. Meren noted in her log that the harbor-wall lantern-tending had performed exactly as the village's purpose required.

### Day 620 of the corpus: a routine summer night

A clear summer night in early Year 2 summer. Moth-counts on the Cliff Path were strong — averaging twenty-eight per lantern. Sky-cats descended on four stretches and reduced moth-counts on those stretches to between eight and twelve by the third watch.

Tessa's morning feather collection that day yielded thirty-five feathers. The autumn store was building.

Hesper reported a strong distillation that day — four vessels produced. Cauldron Hall had not seen four-vessel days since before the Year 1 cold snaps.

The village was, by most measures, recovered.

### Day 640 of the corpus: a Council note

Old Vesh, at the next Council meeting, observed that the village had passed through a complete cycle: a year that had begun thin, become near-bad in mid-winter, recovered through spring, and was now producing well. The Council took the observation as a moment for collective relief.

Meren noted that the lessons of Year 1 had been incorporated into Year 2's policies — the brighter-station policy, the more careful Stilllight substitute training, the more thorough feather-collection. Old Vesh added that incorporating lessons into policy was the village's chief mechanism for surviving across generations, and had been since the worst winter sixty-three years ago.

The Council closed the meeting with the village's customary phrase: "The lanterns must burn." Coraline, sitting in attendance at Council for the first time as a Keeper rather than as an apprentice, repeated the phrase quietly with the others.

### Day 670 of the corpus: a wintergloss test

Bevin Coombe requested permission from the Council to test wintergloss at the Stilllight for one specific summer night when sky-cats had been observed hunting in the cove on three consecutive previous nights. The Council approved the test.

Bevin burned a small quantity of wintergloss alongside the Stilllight's mire-essence at sundown. The lantern produced its usual mist-light, but moth-counts at the Stilllight that night were three rather than the typical twelve to fifteen. The sky-cats did not descend into the cove. The wraith risk for the next lunar cycle was unaffected.

Bevin reported to Council that the test confirmed the expected wintergloss behavior: moths repelled, sky-cats kept away from the cove. The Council recorded the test as a routine confirmation of established practice.

### Day 690 of the corpus: a discussion of the Watch's sealed records

At the full-moon Council meeting, Old Vesh raised the question of whether the sealed records in the Watch should be opened, given that the village had stabilized and the senior Keepers might benefit from reviewing the older deliberations stored there.

The Council debated the question at length. Brindle Coombe argued that sealed records had been sealed for a reason and that opening them now was unnecessary. Meren agreed cautiously. Old Vesh acknowledged the argument but noted that the village's institutional memory was being shaped only by what was openly accessible.

The Council declined to open the sealed records at this meeting. The matter was tabled. Old Vesh asked that the question be revisited at the autumn-equinox meeting. The Council agreed to revisit.

### Day 705 of the corpus: an unexpected feather find

Tessa Brae's morning patrol on day 705 found an unusual quantity of feathers at the cliff base — sixty-three in total, nearly double the usual count for that week. The cause was unclear; no unusually heavy sky-cat hunt had been observed the previous night.

Tessa speculated that several sky-cats had molted simultaneously, possibly a coincidence of seasonal timing among multiple older birds. The Council recorded the find and added the feathers to the store. The autumn feather store was now well above projection.
