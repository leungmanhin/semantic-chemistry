# World Rules

Thirty load-bearing rules of the Lantern-Keepers' world, written in the
controlled language of `style_guide.md` (LAW register): each rule is stated as
a When-conditional, restated as an If-variant with a bounded verb variant, and
completed by generic / copular / negated-generic facts. The designed
paraphrase families are enumerated in `expected_consolidation_map.md`. Lines
beginning with `>` are design annotations and are not part of the parseable
corpus.

## R1 — Lighting a lantern produces mist-light

When a lantern burns mire-essence, the lantern produces mist-light. If a
lantern burns mire-essence, the lantern gives mist-light. Mist-light is a pale
silver flame. Ordinary oil does not produce mist-light.

## R2 — Mist-light attracts nightmoths

When a lantern shows mist-light at night, the mist-light attracts nightmoths.
If a lantern shows mist-light at night, the mist-light draws nightmoths.
Nightmoths do not see ordinary firelight. Nightmoths rarely fly on cold
nights.

## R3 — Nightmoths landing on lanterns leave silken threads

When a nightmoth lands on a lantern, the nightmoth leaves silken threads on
the lantern. If a nightmoth rests on a lantern, the nightmoth sheds silken
threads onto the lantern. Silken threads come from the undersides of
nightmoths.

## R4 — Silken threads boiled in sea-water yield mire-essence

When the Keepers boil silken threads in sea-water, mire-essence forms in the
cauldron. If the Keepers boil silken threads in sea-water, the cauldron yields
mire-essence. The Keepers take the sea-water from the Salt-bloom Tide-pools.
Fresh water does not yield mire-essence.

## R5 — Stored mire-essence feeds future lighting

When mire-essence forms in the cauldron, the Keepers store the mire-essence in
clay vessels. If mire-essence forms in the cauldron, the Keepers keep the
mire-essence in clay vessels. The Keepers draw stored mire-essence when they
tend lanterns. Stored mire-essence feeds future lantern lighting.

> Cycle A closes here: lantern → mist-light → moths → threads → mire-essence → lantern.

## R6 — A crowd of nightmoths brings sky-cats down

When many nightmoths gather at a lantern-row, sky-cats descend from the
cliff-spires. If many nightmoths crowd a lantern-row, sky-cats descend from
the cliff-spires. When few nightmoths gather at a lantern-row, sky-cats stay
on the cliff-spires.

> Threshold (design value, not corpus): "many" ≈ more than twenty moths per lantern-row section per night.

## R7 — Sky-cats hunting reduces nightmoth populations

When sky-cats descend, the sky-cats hunt nightmoths. When sky-cats hunt
nightmoths, the nightmoth crowd at that lantern-row shrinks. If sky-cats hunt
nightmoths, the nightmoth crowd at that lantern-row thins. After a sky-cat
hunt, the thread harvest at that lantern-row falls.

## R8 — Hunted nightmoths flee to dimmer lights

When sky-cats hunt at a lantern-row, the surviving nightmoths flee toward
dimmer lanterns. If sky-cats hunt at a lantern-row, the surviving nightmoths
move to the Hollows. When fleeing nightmoths reach the Hollows, the Hollows
lanterns gain extra silken threads.

## R9 — Sky-cats roost on the cliff-spires by day

Sky-cats roost on the cliff-spires during the day. Sky-cats do not enter the
village. The Keepers tolerate sky-cats.

## R10 — Sky-cats molt feathers to the cliff base

When sky-cats molt, feathers fall to the cliff base. If sky-cats molt, the
sky-cats shed feathers onto the cliff base. Apprentices collect the fallen
feathers in the morning. The Keepers store the collected feathers in a bin at
the Watch.

## R11 — Untended lanterns die before dawn

When a Keeper leaves a lantern untended overnight, the lantern dies before
dawn. If a lantern stays untended through the night, the lantern goes out
before dawn. A Keeper refreshes the mire-essence and trims the wick during the
night.

## R12 — Dead lanterns at the Sunken Cove raise wraiths

When a lantern at the Sunken Cove dies before dawn, a wraith emerges from the
cove water. If a lantern at the Sunken Cove goes out at night, a wraith rises
from the cove water. Lanterns elsewhere do not raise wraiths when they die.
The Sunken Cove is the village's drowned graveyard.

## R13 — Wraiths drain mire-essence from lit lanterns

When a wraith approaches a lit lantern, the wraith drains mire-essence from
the lantern. If a wraith nears a lit lantern, the wraith pulls mire-essence
from the lantern. A drained lantern burns dimly.

## R14 — Drained lanterns give weak mist-light

When a wraith drains a lantern, the lantern gives weak mist-light. If a wraith
drains a lantern, the lantern shows dim mist-light. Weak mist-light rarely
attracts nightmoths. When lanterns attract few nightmoths, the thread harvest
falls.

> Cycle D closes here: untended → wraith → drained → weak mist-light → less production.

## R15 — Ground feathers ward lanterns from wraiths

When the Keepers scatter ground feathers around a lit lantern, the feathers
ward the lantern from wraiths. If the Keepers spread ground feathers around a
lit lantern, the feathers shield the lantern from wraiths. Wraiths do not
approach a warded lantern. Fresh ground feathers ward a lantern. Stale ground
feathers do not ward a lantern.

> Cycle C hinge: the regulator's byproduct (feathers) protects production in the wraith zone.

## R16 — Cold winds extinguish unprepared lanterns

When cold wind blows off the sea at night, the cold wind extinguishes
unprepared lanterns. If cold wind strikes the Cliff Path at night, unprepared
lanterns go out. Lanterns in shielded coves stay safe from cold winds.

## R17 — Salt-bloom in winter slows the burn

When the Keepers add salt-bloom to mire-essence in winter, the mire-essence
burns slowly. If the Keepers mix salt-bloom into mire-essence in winter, the
mire-essence burns slowly. A salt-bloomed lantern resists cold wind.

## R18 — Salt-bloom is harvested at new moon

When the Keepers harvest salt-bloom at the new moon, the salt-bloom keeps its
potency. If the Keepers gather salt-bloom at the new moon, the salt-bloom
stays potent. When the Keepers gather salt-bloom at other moon phases, the
salt-bloom turns to chalky residue.

## R19 — Spring tides bloom salt-bloom in the tide-pools

When a spring tide fills the tide-pools, salt-bloom blooms in the tide-pools.
If a spring tide fills the tide-pools, salt-bloom grows in the tide-pools.
Salt-bloom is a pale crystalline crust. The bloom soon dissolves into the
seawater.

## R20 — Spring tides occur near new moon and full moon

Spring tides occur near the new moon. Spring tides occur near the full moon.
The Keepers time the salt-bloom harvest to the new-moon spring tide.

## R21 — Wintergloss repels nightmoths

When wintergloss burns in a lantern, the wintergloss repels nightmoths from
that lantern. If wintergloss burns alongside mire-essence, nightmoths avoid
that lantern. The Keepers rarely burn wintergloss. The Keepers sometimes burn
wintergloss at the Stilllight Lantern.

## R22 — Cliff-spires shield the Northcove

When cliff-spires shield a cove from north winds, lanterns in that cove burn
stably through winter. If cliff-spires block the north wind from a cove,
lanterns in that cove stay lit through winter. The Northcove is the shielded
cove of Aelmere. Northcove lanterns never go out in cold winds.

## R23 — Keepers inherit lantern-stations

When a senior Keeper retires, an heir inherits the lantern-station. If a
senior Keeper dies, an heir inherits the lantern-station. An inherited station
stays in the family. The Council must approve any station transfer.

## R24 — Newcomers apprentice under a senior Keeper

A newcomer must apprentice under a senior Keeper. A new apprentice does not
tend a lantern alone. The Council permits an apprentice to begin solo tending.

## R25 — Sustained solo tending completes apprenticeship

When an apprentice completes many solo nights of tending, the Council invests
the apprentice as a Keeper. If the apprentice's lantern fails, the solo count
resets. If the apprentice's lantern dims, the solo count resets.

> Design value (not corpus): "many solo nights" = thirty consecutive nights.

## R26 — The Council convenes at full moon

When the moon is full, the Council convenes at the Watch. If the moon is full,
the Council meets at the Watch. The Council rarely meets at other times.

## R27 — The Council redistributes mire-essence

When the Council convenes, the Council redistributes mire-essence across the
lantern-stations. If the Council meets, the Council reallocates mire-essence
among the lantern-stations. Productive stations contribute mire-essence to a
central pool. Burdened stations draw mire-essence from the central pool. The
Keepers record the redistribution in the Watch ledgers.

## R28 — Summer hunting yields the autumn feather store

When sky-cats hunt often through summer, many feathers accumulate at the cliff
base by autumn. If sky-cats hunt often in summer, the autumn feather store
grows. Frequent hunting causes frequent molting.

## R29 — Large feather stores enable safe Sunken-Cove tending

When the feather store at the Watch is large, the Keepers tend the Sunken Cove
lanterns safely in winter. If the Watch holds a large feather store, winter
tending at the Sunken Cove stays safe. The Council issues feather rations to
the Sunken-Cove station in winter. When the feather store is small, wraith
risk grows.

## R30 — Wraith activity prompts feather-ration requests

When wraith activity at the Sunken Cove increases, the neighboring stations
request feather rations from the Council. If wraith sightings at the Sunken
Cove rise, the stations ask the Council for feather rations. The Council
weighs the requests against the central feather store. The Council prioritizes
stations with drained lanterns.
